# DatePicker

A date field with a calendar behind it.

```tsx
<DatePicker name="departure" />
```

<Canvas of={DatePickerStories.Default} />

## Why it exists

Its three parts are all exported, and for a while the docs said to assemble them
yourself. Then they were assembled, and the recipe turned out to have **five
steps that each fail silently when missed**:

1. write the field with `writeDateInput`, never `carrier.value = …` — a plain
   assignment updates the DOM and tells nobody, because React's value tracker
   absorbs it;
2. pass `onDateChange` back, or a date TYPED into the field never reaches the
   grid and the popover reopens on the day chosen three edits ago;
3. move `month` with the value, or that day is one the grid does not draw and
   the popover opens with nothing selected at all;
4. do not nest a `Button` inside `PopoverTrigger`, which is one;
5. in a form, `carrierRef` belongs to the binding, so the write goes through the
   library's own lever.

Three of those were got wrong by the people who wrote the parts, on first
assembly, in one afternoon (ADR-0027's amendment records it). This component is
those five steps, once.

**It hides nothing.** `DateInput`, `Calendar` and `Popover` stay exported and
stay documented. A shape this does not offer — a controlled open state, a second
trigger, a calendar somewhere other than a popover — is still assembled by hand
exactly as before.

## Props

<Controls of={DatePickerStories.Default} />

Everything `DateInput` takes, plus what a grid needs. There is no `value` prop
and no `open` prop, and both absences are deliberate: the browser owns the value
(ADR-0013), so seed with `defaultValue` (ISO) or `defaultDate` and read through
`onDateChange`; and an open state nobody but the trigger touches is state the
component can keep.

## Usage

### Typed, or chosen

<Canvas of={DatePickerStories.ReadOnly} />

`readOnly` flips which surface opens the calendar, and the rule behind it is not
a preference:

- **a field that can be typed into has a caret to protect**, so the trigger is a
  separate target — React Aria, MUI desktop and the APG's own date-picker example
  all do this;
- **a field that cannot has nothing to protect**, so the whole field is the
  target — which is what Fluent does, and it does it precisely because its field
  is `allowTextInput: false`.

**Which one to pick is a question about the date, not about the design.** A date
the user KNOWS — a birth date, an expiry, an invoice date — is faster typed than
navigated; GOV.UK measured a calendar as actively worse there, and their
"memorable date" pattern has no calendar at all. A date the user CHOOSES from
what is available — a booking, an appointment, a flight — is the Airbnb case:
typing means nothing, because the answer is in the grid next to the days that are
taken.

Nothing else changes. The trigger stays, because it is what a keyboard and a
screen reader reach; the value is still submitted, unlike a disabled control's;
and the field stays focusable and selectable, so the date can still be read and
copied. `readOnly` is the platform's own attribute doing its own job — the
browser refuses the typing, and this component only stops claiming otherwise: the
`gg/mm/aaaa` hint is an instruction, so it goes from the placeholder and from the
field's description together.

**It is not styled as "disabled", deliberately.** A read-only field elsewhere
might reasonably look inert; here the field is the thing to click, and dimming it
would say "not available" where it means "open the calendar". It keeps full
contrast and takes a pointer cursor.

### Per-date rules

<Canvas of={DatePickerStories.PerDateRules} />

`isDateDisabled` is the reason the calendar exists at all. `min`/`max` on the
platform's date input are an interval, not a set, so "never on a Sunday, and
these three days are booked" cannot be said to it. A refused day keeps its place
and stays reachable by the arrows — it is `aria-disabled`, not `disabled`, which
is the APG's "focusable but not activatable".

**It refuses days in the grid only.** A date typed into the field passes through
every intermediate value on its way to a valid one, so refusing them as they are
typed would make the field unusable. Validate on submit as you would anyway.

### What it hands back

<Canvas of={DatePickerStories.ReadingTheValue} />

A `CivilDate` — `{ year, month, day }`, months **1–12** — and never a `Date`. A
`Date` is an instant; a day on a calendar is not. `new Date('2026-08-12')` is the
11th of August in every timezone west of Greenwich, which is the defect this
whole family exists to stop repeating.

`onDateChange` fires from **both** doors — a day clicked in the grid and a date
typed into the field — and only ever on a whole date, so a half-typed field
reports nothing.

What the form posts is different and stays different: an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` on the
carrier, under the field's `name`, as one ordinary entry.

### Locale

<Canvas of={DatePickerStories.AcrossLocales} />

The field's order and separator, the month and weekday names, the digits and the
day a week starts on all come from the locale you gave `UiProvider`. Nothing
here reads the browser's, which is the ceiling that made this family necessary:
a native `<input type="date">` lays its segments out in the BROWSER's locale and
offers no way to be told otherwise (ADR-0027).

### Disabled

<Canvas of={DatePickerStories.Disabled} />

The trigger goes with the field. A calendar that still opened over a field
nobody can type in would offer a way to change a value that is not changeable.

## In a form

Use [FormDatePicker](?path=/docs/components-form-adapters-formdatepicker--docs),
which binds it to the form adapter in scope — and needs no library-specific code
to get the calendar's choice into the form, because it holds the carrier itself.

Unbound, this component is still a perfectly ordinary field: it posts under its
`name`, comes back from `form.reset()`, and can be read with `FormData`.

## Accessibility

- **One tab stop for the field, one for the trigger**, and nothing nested inside
  either. `PopoverTrigger` is itself a `Button`, so it takes the label directly.
- **The trigger has a name in words** — the design system's own copy in the
  declared locale, or your `triggerLabel`. A glyph is not a label: without one it
  would announce as "button" and nothing else.
- **The glyph is drawn here and `aria-hidden`**, in `currentColor`. An icon set
  is brand identity and lives app-side; a picker whose trigger was blank until an
  app injected one would break on somebody else's configuration. Pass `icon` to
  use your own.
- **The grid is the APG's date grid**: a month is one tab stop, the arrows move
  by day and week, PageUp/PageDown by month, and the focused cell is a
  `<td role="gridcell">` carrying the focus, the name and `aria-selected`
  together.
- **The field announces its format** through its description, so a screen-reader
  user is told the order before typing rather than after the mask surprises them.
