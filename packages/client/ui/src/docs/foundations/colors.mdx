# Colors

Colour here is a **role**, never a hue. A component asks for
`--fm-color-primary-subtle-foreground` — "the ink that goes on the soft primary
wash" — and a theme decides what that is. That indirection is the whole point:
a rebrand moves a value in one stylesheet instead of every call site, and the
component keeps working because it never knew the colour in the first place.

There **is** a palette underneath, and it is not yours to reach for. The
contract exposes semantic roles and nothing else: the ramp exists so the roles
can be derived from something instead of each carrying its own number, not so a
component can ask for `primary-500`. See [Three levels](#three-levels) for what
sits where.

Everything below is derived: the names come from `@fmmenchi/tokens`, and the
values are read out of the DOM in whichever theme is on screen. Use the
**Theme** toolbar — every swatch and every number on this page follows it,
because nothing here holds a copy of a value.

## The vocabulary

Every family is built from the same few words. Learn these six and you can read
any role in the system without looking it up.

| Role             | What it is                                      | Use it for                                                                              |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `base`           | The solid fill. The loudest form of the family. | A filled button, a solid badge, a filled progress bar. One per screen area, not five.   |
| `on base`        | The ink that sits **on** the solid fill.        | Label and icons inside that filled thing. Never as text on the page.                    |
| `subtle`         | The soft wash — same meaning, a tenth as loud.  | Alert backgrounds, chips, selected rows, anything that tints a region behind real text. |
| `on subtle`      | The ink that sits **on** the wash.              | Text inside the alert or the chip. It is dark where `on base` is light.                 |
| `border`         | The line at the edge of a subtle region.        | The rule around an alert. Status families only — an action family has hover instead.    |
| `hover`/`active` | The fill, darkened one and two steps.           | Pointer and pressed states of a `base` fill. Never a resting colour.                    |

Two rules that follow from the shape rather than from taste:

**An ink is only valid on its own fill.** `on base` is measured against `base`
and nothing else. Put it on the page background and the contrast guarantee in
the table below does not apply to it — most `on base` inks here are white.

**`base` and `subtle` are not light and dark versions of each other.** They are
two different jobs: `base` is a thing you press, `subtle` is a region you read
inside. Choosing between them is choosing whether the colour is the object or
the backdrop — which is why each has its own ink instead of sharing one.

`disabled` and `on disabled` mirror `base`/`on base` for the unavailable state,
and are the one pair exempt from the contrast floor (see below).

## The matrix

Families down, slots across — the one view that lets a colour be compared with
the colour it should match. Reading **down** a column puts every family's
`subtle` side by side; reading **across** a row is one family's full vocabulary.

The empty cells carry as much as the filled ones. `border` is blank for the four
action families and filled for the status ones; `hover` and `active` are the
reverse. That is the difference between something you press and something you
read, drawn rather than described.

Hover any cell for its role, its property and its resolved value.

## Three levels

Colour is built in three, and knowing which one you are looking at is most of
understanding the system.

**1 · Bases.** One colour per family — `--fm-palette-primary-base`. Seven
numbers, and the only place a hue is decided.

**2 · The palette.** A `100…900` ramp derived from each base, in CSS:

```css
--fm-palette-primary-800: oklch(
  from var(--fm-palette-primary-base) calc(l - 0.24) calc(c * 0.72) h
);
```

**3 · Roles.** The 84 semantic tokens, each pointing at a step —
`--fm-color-primary: var(--fm-palette-primary-700)`.

**Components only ever touch level 3.** The palette is not part of the contract:
it has no `@property` registration, no Tailwind bridge, and no promise of
stability. Reach past a role and you are depending on something that can move
without notice.

### Why the ramp looks the way it does

Neither curve was chosen — both were measured, and they are the interesting part
of this page.

**The lightness curve was already there.** Before any of this existed, every
family's roles sat at the same offsets from its fill: hover −0.10, active −0.19,
subtle +0.49, disabled +0.39. Step for step, all seven. The ramp methodology the
old tokens described really had been applied by hand; the bases are just those
fills undone, which is why step 700 reproduces the fill each family always had.

**The chroma coefficients _are_ the sRGB gamut.** Solved numerically, the most
the tightest hue can take at each lightness is `100 ×0.22`, `200 ×0.55`,
`700 ×0.96`, `800 ×0.72`, `900 ×0.50` — and those land within a hundredth of the
literals the system already shipped. The numbers that looked irregular were not
untidiness: whoever picked them had pushed every step to the edge of what sRGB
can display. Borrowing another system's curve instead put five blue steps _out_
of gamut, which is how this was discovered.

There is **no step 50**: at base + 0.42 the lightness is ≈0.97, where the gamut
admits almost nothing — every family solved under 0.10 chroma, `warning` at
0.00. A step that can only be grey is not a step of a colour ramp.

One curve serves every family, and that costs something: `accent` and `success`
could carry more chroma at 100 than the red allows, and do not. That is the
price of a constant step — and a constant step is what makes this a scale
instead of a set of values with a shared prefix.

### What is not converted yet

**The dark preset still holds literals.** Levelling it too produced 20 contrast
regressions in the browser suite — `menu` in dark measured 4.13 against a 4.5
floor — because reducing chroma to fit the gamut moves relative luminance.
Retuning that is measurement, not guesswork, so it is a separate change. Until
then the two files are asymmetric: light derives, dark states.

**Relative colour is a waiver, not the default tier.** It is Baseline _Newly_
and reaches _Widely_ around January 2027; the platform's floor is Widely
(ADR-0017), and this takes the same kind of waiver the Popover API did — not
degradable, but the Widely date lands before any consumer could realistically
ship. If that stops being true, the documented way back is a precomputed ramp:
same three levels, literals instead of the formula.

## The palette

Level 2 as it is declared: every family's ramp, constant step, palest first. The
step names are read out of `vars.css` itself, so this cannot drift from the
stylesheet — and the values come from the DOM, because the declaration is a
formula rather than a colour.

Two things worth noticing. `destructive` and `error` are the **same ramp**: same
hue, same base, two names because one is a button and the other is a message.
And the neutrals are their own shape — ten rungs including `0`, `25` and `750`,
written by hand rather than derived, because surfaces need steps a hundredth
apart at the pale end that no single curve produces.

## Where the roles land

Below is what those ramps actually resolve to: every distinct colour the
theme paints, grouped by hue and ordered by lightness. Read from the DOM rather
than from the ramp's source, so it shows the colours after the browser has
evaluated the formulas — and it cannot go stale, because it _is_ the tokens.

It also answers a question the ramp cannot: which roles land on the **same**
value, and therefore move together when a base moves.

Each strip is named after the family with the most roles in that hue —
`destructive`, not `hue 27°` — because the angle is where a colour sits, not
what it is for. Anything else living at the same angle is listed as **shared
with**, and the distinction is worth keeping: sitting at the same hue is not
being the same role.

The blue strip is the one to read closely, because the three names sharing it
are in three different situations:

- **`secondary` is a genuinely different colour.** One degree away, but chroma
  `0.05` against `primary`'s `0.135` — near enough to grey that it reads as
  slate, not blue.
- **`link` is `primary`, to the last decimal.** Both are
  `oklch(41% 0.135 255)`, and `link-hover` equals `primary-hover`. It is not a
  new colour; it is a second name for one, so that a link can stop following the
  brand later without every call site changing.
- **`selection` is `primary-subtle`**, and `selection-foreground` is
  `primary-hover`. Same story.

None of that is visible in the role listing, where the four appear as unrelated
entries. It is the reason to draw a palette at all.

Under each swatch is its **lightness**, 0–100. A `·N` after it means **N roles
resolve to that exact value**: the 84 roles come out as far fewer colours, and
in the light theme a single value serves eleven of them. Hovering a swatch lists
every role that shares it.

The counts are the theme's own — a preset is a complete reassignment of every
role, so toggling redraws this whole section rather than re-tinting it. In light
the lightest shade is pure white and does most of that sharing; in dark it is
not, and the sharing lands elsewhere.

Two things this view makes visible that a list of roles cannot:

- the neutrals are by far the longest ramp, which is what a restrained system
  looks like from the inside;
- there are **two blues, ten degrees apart** — `info` sits at 245° while
  `primary`, `link`, `ring` and `selection` sit at 255°. It holds in both
  presets, and it is the kind of thing that is invisible until the palette is
  drawn.

## Action families

Four families for things a person can do — `primary`, `secondary`, `accent`,
`destructive` — each with the same eight roles: the solid fill, the ink on it,
hover and active, the subtle wash and its ink, and the disabled pair.

A family is complete or it is not a family: a theme that assigns six of the
eight has a component with nowhere to go on hover.

<Canvas of={ColorStories.Action} />

## Status families

`success`, `warning`, `info`, `error` — feedback, not action. They carry a
`-border` instead of hover and active states, because a status is something a
person reads, not something they press.

`error` and `destructive` are the same red and deliberately two roles: one is an
alert, the other is a button. Splitting them is what lets a destructive button
darken on press while an error message stays exactly as loud as it was.

## Surfaces, inputs and neutrals

What is left: the page and its cards, the input palette, the neutral and
disabled roles, and the single-purpose ones — `ring`, `scrim`, `selection`,
`link`, `tooltip`.

These are grouped by name prefix rather than by a list written here, so the
shape you see is the shape the contract has. Some come out as a pair, some as a
strip of one — and a strip of one is a colour that was needed once and named,
not a gap waiting to be filled.

## Contrast, measured

Every pair the contract declares, with the ratio measured live — the ink
painted on its own background and the two read back, so the number is the
browser's answer rather than this page's arithmetic.

The floor is **4.5**, WCAG 1.4.3 at AA for body text. Disabled pairs are marked
exempt: 1.4.3 excludes them, and they are shown anyway, because a number nobody
is allowed to hide is how you notice one that has drifted well past "low on
purpose".

An exempt pair is sampled as two tiles rather than as a line of text, and that
is not a cosmetic choice. The exemption covers the colours of a disabled
_control_; it does not cover this page painting real 1.38-contrast text in a
table. The first version did exactly that and axe failed the story — correctly,
which is the argument for running the a11y check on the documentation and not
only on the components.

This is not the gate. `tokens.test.ts` already fails the build on a pair that
drops below the floor, in both presets. What the table adds is the distance: a
pair that passes at 4.6 and one that passes at 12 are the same green tick to a
test and very different things to look at — and toggling the theme re-measures
every row.

The pairs are derived by rule (`role` has a partner when `role-foreground`
exists), which finds all of them but one: `background`/`foreground` are the page
itself, named before that convention existed. It is the only hand-stated pair
here.
