# Colors

Colour is built in **three levels**, and they are the order of this page:

1. **Bases** — one colour per family. Eight numbers.
2. **The palette** — a ramp derived from each base, `100…900`.
3. **Roles** — the 84 semantic tokens, each pointing at a step.

A component only ever touches level 3. It asks for
`--fm-color-primary-subtle-foreground` — "the ink that goes on the soft primary
wash" — and the theme decides what that is. That indirection is the whole point:
a rebrand moves a base instead of every call site, and the component keeps
working because it never knew the colour.

Everything below is read live: the names come from the stylesheet and the
contract, the values from the DOM in whichever theme is on screen. Use the
**Theme** toolbar — every swatch and every number here follows it, because
nothing holds a copy of a value.

## Level 1 · Bases

One colour per family, and the only place a hue is decided. Everything else is
derived from these eight or points at something that is.

This is also what a brand preset replaces. An app bringing its own colours
overrides the bases and inherits the ramp and the mapping — eight numbers
instead of eighty-four.

## Level 2 · The palette

Each base becomes a ramp: nine steps, constant spacing, `100` palest to `900`
darkest, with `500` being the base itself. The neutrals are their own scale —
rungs 50 apart, from white (`0`) to black (`1000`).

<Canvas of={ColorStories.Ramps} />

Two things it shows that a list of roles cannot. `destructive` and `error` are
the **same ramp** — same hue, same base, two names because one is a button and
the other is a message. And the neutral scale is **shared between the themes**:
light and dark do not have different greys, they point at opposite ends of one
scale, `background` being `neutral-15` in light and `neutral-790` in dark.

### Why the ramp looks the way it does

Neither curve was chosen. Both were measured, and that is the interesting part.

**The lightness curve was already there.** Before any of this existed, every
family's roles sat at the same offsets from its fill: hover −0.10, active −0.19,
subtle +0.49, disabled +0.39. Step for step, all eight families. So the bases
are simply those fills undone, which is why step 700 reproduces the fill each
family always had.

**The chroma coefficients _are_ the sRGB gamut.** Solved numerically, the most
the tightest hue can take at each lightness is `100 ×0.22`, `200 ×0.55`,
`700 ×0.96`, `800 ×0.72`, `900 ×0.50` — within a hundredth of the literals the
system already shipped. The numbers that looked irregular were not untidiness:
every step had been pushed to the edge of what sRGB can display. Borrowing
another system's curve put five blue steps _out_ of gamut, which is how this was
found.

There is **no step 50**: at base + 0.42 the lightness is ≈0.97, where the gamut
admits almost nothing — every family solved under 0.10 chroma, `warning` at
0.00. A step that can only be grey is not a step of a colour ramp.

One curve serves every family, and it costs something: `accent` and `success`
could carry more chroma at 100 than the red allows, and do not. That is the
price of a constant step, and a constant step is what makes this a scale.

The greys are the exception, written out rather than derived: no curve coarse
enough to span 1.00 to 0.00 also resolves the pale end, where a page, a card and
a wash sit a hundredth of a lightness apart. Their cast deepens with the grey —
0 at white, 0.004 at 98.5%, 0.02 from 46% down — because that is where the gamut
leaves room.

## Level 3 · Roles

What a component may actually use. Every family is built from the same few
words, and learning these six is enough to read any role in the system.

| Role             | What it is                                      | Use it for                                                                            |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `base`           | The solid fill. The loudest form of the family. | A filled button, a solid badge, a filled progress bar. One per screen area, not five. |
| `on base`        | The ink that sits **on** the solid fill.        | Label and icons inside that filled thing. Never as text on the page.                  |
| `subtle`         | The soft wash — same meaning, a tenth as loud.  | Alert backgrounds, chips, selected rows: anything tinting a region behind real text.  |
| `on subtle`      | The ink that sits **on** the wash.              | Text inside the alert or the chip. It is dark where `on base` is light.               |
| `border`         | The line at the edge of a subtle region.        | The rule around an alert. Status families only — an action family has hover instead.  |
| `hover`/`active` | The fill, darkened one and two steps.           | Pointer and pressed states of a `base` fill. Never a resting colour.                  |

Two rules follow from the shape rather than from taste.

**An ink is only valid on its own fill.** `on base` is measured against `base`
and nothing else. Put it on the page background and the contrast guarantee below
does not apply — most `on base` inks here are white.

**`base` and `subtle` are not light and dark versions of each other.** They are
two jobs: `base` is a thing you press, `subtle` is a region you read inside.
Choosing between them is choosing whether the colour is the object or the
backdrop, which is why each has its own ink instead of sharing one.

`disabled` and `on disabled` mirror `base`/`on base` for the unavailable state,
and are the one pair exempt from the contrast floor.

### The matrix

Families down, slots across — the view that lets a colour be compared with the
colour it should match. Reading **down** a column puts every family's `subtle`
side by side; reading **across** a row is one family's full vocabulary. Hover
any cell for its role, its property and its resolved value.

The empty cells carry as much as the filled ones: `border` is blank for the four
action families and filled for the status ones, while `hover` and `active` are
the reverse. That is the difference between something you press and something
you read, drawn rather than described.

### Surfaces, inputs and neutrals

What has no family shape: the page and its cards, the input palette, and the
single-purpose roles — `ring`, `scrim`, `selection`, `link`, `tooltip`.

`input` is not a row in the matrix because its slots are its own (`invalid`,
`placeholder`), and a column used by one family makes every other row look
incomplete.

## Contrast, measured

Every pair the contract declares, with the ratio measured live — the ink painted
on its own background and the two read back, so the number is the browser's
answer rather than this page's arithmetic.

The floor is **4.5**, WCAG 1.4.3 at AA for body text. Disabled pairs are marked
exempt: 1.4.3 excludes them, and they are shown anyway, because a number nobody
is allowed to hide is how you notice one that has drifted well past "low on
purpose".

An exempt pair is sampled as two tiles rather than as a line of text, and that
is not cosmetic. The exemption covers the colours of a disabled _control_; it
does not cover this page painting real 1.38-contrast text in a table. The first
version did exactly that and axe failed the story — correctly, which is the
argument for running the a11y check over the documentation and not only over the
components.

This is not the gate. `tokens.test.ts` already fails the build on a pair that
drops below the floor, in both presets, and now does it on the _derived_ values.
What the table adds is the distance: a pair that passes at 4.6 and one that
passes at 12 are the same green tick to a test and very different things to look
at — and toggling the theme re-measures every row.

The pairs are derived by rule (`role` has a partner when `role-foreground`
exists), which finds all of them but one: `background`/`foreground` are the page
itself, named before that convention existed.

## What is not converted yet

**The dark preset is half done.** Its neutrals are levelled — 27 roles point at
the shared scale, and nothing moved, because a grey carries almost no chroma and
so no gamut risk. Its colour families still hold literals: levelling those
produced 20 contrast regressions in the browser suite (`menu` in dark measured
4.13 against the 4.5 floor), because reducing chroma to fit the gamut moves
relative luminance. Retuning that is measurement, not guesswork, so it is a
separate change.

**Relative colour is a waiver, not the default tier.** It is Baseline _Newly_
and reaches _Widely_ around January 2027; the platform floor is Widely
(ADR-0017), and this takes the same kind of waiver the Popover API did — not
degradable, but the Widely date lands before any consumer could realistically
ship. If that stops being true, the documented way back is a precomputed ramp:
same three levels, literals instead of the formula.
